# Micro drill press controller — plan

Closed-loop spindle controller for a 20k rpm micro-drilling press. RPM is the control variable, load torque is compensated as fast as possible, and the controller actively protects the bits.

---

## 1. Goals

- Hold the RPM setpoint under varying drilling torque, reacting within about 1 ms.
- Prevent micro bits from snapping: monitor chip load and torque.
- Measure table height for depth readout and feed-rate sensing.
- Local UI: 2.0" SPI TFT plus a rotary encoder.

## 2. Open questions (answer before schematic)

- [x] Motor voltage: 24 V
- [x] Motor terminal resistance: R ≈ 4.66 Ω (lowest of 4 multimeter readings 4.74–7.42 Ω, minus 0.083 Ω probe resistance, 2026-11-21). Stall current ≈ 24 V / 4.66 Ω ≈ 5.2 A (cold winding)
- [ ] Motor no-load and drilling current, and inductance L. Deferred: not safe to run the motor loose on the bench. Measure in the press via IPROPI logging (phase 1)
- [x] Rear shaft: Ø5 mm, about 8 mm free
- [x] Table: lever + link (not rack and pinion), table on vertical guide rods, central post through the base, about 50 mm travel
- [ ] Bit range to support (e.g. 0.2–1.0 mm)
- [x] Supply: external 24 V PSU, 5.5×2.5 mm plug, centre positive. Not bought yet; buy a regulated 24 V brick of at least 6 A (150 W) whose output stays ≤ 25 V and that has short-circuit/overcurrent protection (hiccup or foldback); the board has no input fuse (#38). A lab supply must have its current limit set to 6 A or less. Bench: plug into J1 on the board. Final install: panel-mount jack on the front, wired to the J11 screw terminal

## 3. System architecture

```
 AS5047P (spindle angle, ABI) ──┐
 Table height sensor ───────────┼──> STM32G4 ──> Motor driver ──> Spindle motor
 Rotary encoder (+ button) ─────┘       │   ^          │
                                        │   └──────────┘ current sense (IPROPI / shunt)
                                        └──> TFT display (SPI1, shared with AS5047P)
                                        └──> Buzzer
```

### 4.2 Sensor supply

- AS5047P: 3.3 V operation is supported. Tie VDD and VDD3V3 together; VDD3V3 range is 3.0–3.6 V (D60 p6, p10). ABI and UVW outputs: 1 mA max and 50 pF max capacitive load (D60 p6). Keep the cable short.
- AS5311: 3.3 V operation is supported. Bypass the LDO by tying VDD3V3 to VDD5V. VDD3V3 still needs its capacitor, and I/O levels follow VDD5V (D61 p17–18).
- Result: no 5 V rail is needed. Both sensor cables carry 3.3 V and GND.
- AS5047P TEST pin to GND (D60 p3). AS5311 Prog to VSS, NC pins left open (D61 p5).
- **AS5311 CSn unlocks the A/B/Index outputs once, at power-up** (#46). D61 p5 says CSn "must be low to enable incremental outputs"; D61 p12 (7.1.1) explains this: with CSn high or open at power-up, A, B and Index stay high until CSn goes low or gets a low pulse and offset compensation is done. "Once the incremental outputs are unlocked they can not be disabled during operation" (D61 p12). So an SSI read (CSn high → low → high) does not stop A/B/Index, and HEIGHT_CSn can idle high between reads. HEIGHT_CSn has no external pull-down and the AS5311 has an internal ~50 kΩ pull-up (D61 p5), so the part always powers up locked (the "low at power-up" mode needs R_ext ≤ 5 kΩ to GND, D61 p12). Firmware: after boot, pulse HEIGHT_CSn low (a first SSI read does this), wait for the power-up time, then start the TIM4 count. D61 does not say outright that A/B keep updating while an SSI frame is clocked out. Bring-up test: move the table steadily while doing SSI reads at the planned rate, and check that the TIM4 count matches the SSI position with no lost steps. In use, firmware repeats this check on every SSI read (#62, see 4.3).
- Cable pinouts (same pin order on both ends):
  - Spindle J6 (main) ↔ J4 (sensor): custom JLCPCB flex, 12P 0.5 mm, 0.3 mm thick with stiffener; connector HC-FPC-05-20-12RLTAG (C19270548, Extended, double-sided contacts). 1 3V3, 2 GND, 3 SCK (shared SPI1), 4 GND, 5 MOSI (shared SPI1), 6 MISO, 7 CSn, 8 GND, 9 A, 10 B, 11 I, 12 GND. GND pins split the cable (#61): SCK sits between two GNDs, and GND 8 separates the SPI group from A/B/I, so display traffic on SCK/MOSI does not couple into CSn or the encoder lines. Same pin order at both ends (pin N to pin N); contact side and mirroring for the flex are still open in #4.
  - Height J7 (main) ↔ J5 (sensor): JST-XH 8P, B8B-XH-2 (C157973, Extended, THT), hand-crimped cable. 1 3V3, 2 GND, 3 CSn, 4 CLK, 5 DO, 6 A, 7 B, 8 I
    - One GND (#62). Pin 2 carries the AS5311 supply current (21 mA max, D61 p7) and the return current of CSn, CLK, DO, A, B and I. The height count is incremental, so a glitch on A or B stays as a height error. Cable: twist all 8 wires together as one bundle (or use a round 8-core cable), so GND runs next to every signal for the whole length; do not run loose single wires. A flat ribbon in pin order also works, but it puts GND 4–5 wires from A/B, so the twisted bundle is better. Cable length is still open (#47); keep it short. Firmware adds the TIM4 input filter and the SSI cross-check (4.3).
    - Second GND pin: not added (#62). No Basic or Preferred 9-pin JST-XH exists: B9B-XH-A (C157966) and B9B-XH-2 (C157967) are both Extended (jlcsearch, 2026-11-23), and neither is used here or in netdaq. Swapping the Extended 8-pin for an Extended 9-pin would add no feeder fee, but it widens J5/J7 by one 2.5 mm pitch and moves pins on both boards. With the twisted bundle, the input filter and the per-read SSI check, the risk is small. Revisit if bring-up shows TIM4/SSI mismatches.
- UI sheet: J8 display FPC (same C19270548 part); R35 100 k pull-up on LCD_CS to +3V3 (C25741, Basic, 0402), so the display stays deselected while PF0 floats in reset and boot and SPI1 talks to the spindle sensor (#32); backlight R16 10 Ω ∥ R26 47 Ω = 8.25 Ω from 3V3 (C22859 + C23182, both Basic 0603; no Basic 8.2 Ω exists) + Q2 AO3400A (C20917, Basic) low side, PWM on LCD_BL; EC11 encoder SW1 with 10 k pull-ups and 10 nF on A/B/SW. SW1 is Alps EC11E09244AQ (C470753, Extended, THT): vertical, 20 mm flat shaft (24.5 mm above the mounting surface), no threaded bushing, 18 detents / 9 pulses, push switch with 1.5 mm travel, rated 10 mA 5 V DC (D74 p1, p3 fig. 5). It is the only EC11E with a switch that LCSC stocks. With TIM3 in x4 encoder mode that is 36 counts per turn, 2 counts per detent. The KiCad footprint RotaryEncoder_Alps_EC11E-Switch_Vertical_H20mm matches fig. 5: pin rows, pitch and lug position are equal; the lug slots sit 0.1 mm further out per side (11.2 mm vs 11.0 mm centre to centre); buzzer BZ1 Jiangsu Huaneng MLT-8530 (C94599, Extended; no Basic or Preferred buzzer exists on JLC) on Q3 AO3400A with D4 1N4148W flyback. It is a magnetic, externally driven SMD buzzer, 8.5 × 8.5 × 3 mm: rated 3.6 V0-p, operating 2.5–4.5 V0-p, resonance 2700 Hz, ≥80 dB at 10 cm and ≤95 mA at 5 V0-p square wave, coil 16 ± 3 Ω (D75 p2). Because it is magnetic, D4 stays. Firmware drives BUZZER (TIM15_CH2) at 2.7 kHz, 50 % duty, and must leave the pin low when idle: held on, the coil draws DC 3.3 V / 13 Ω min ≈ 0.25 A (general knowledge, from the D75 p2 coil resistance). The EasyEDA footprint BUZ-SMD_4P-L8.5-W8.5-P8.50-BR has 2.4 mm pads at ±3.6 mm, against the D75 p4 pattern of 2.3 mm pads at ±3.55 mm; pad 1 = + lead, pad 2 = − lead, pads 3/4 = dummy pads (no net). Pole A: pin 2 (common) to ENABLE_IN, pin 1 to GND, pin 3 and pole B not connected. ENABLE_IN low = run, high = stop, with the 10 k pull-up R22 and 100 nF C31. Mains-style power off is a panel switch in the 24 V lead to J11, not on the board. No e-stop: this is a small benchtop drill. Backlight sizing is general knowledge: (3.3 − V_f) / 8.25 Ω gives ~12–61 mA for V_f 3.2–2.8 V, always under the 80 mA abs max (D67 p5).
- On the sensor sheets, power is the sheet-local nets 3V3_SNS / GND_SNS (with PWR_FLAGs), not the global +3V3/GND. They damp ringing on the flex branch and at the display. The AS5047P needs t_clk ≥ 100 ns, so ≤ 10 MHz (D60 p12); the display may run faster, so firmware must drop the SPI1 clock to ≤ 10 MHz before it pulls SPINDLE_CSn low. 33 Ω is general knowledge for a source series resistor; fit 0 Ω if bring-up shows clean edges. PA5/PA7 float in reset and boot, checked in #68: "During and just after reset, the alternate functions are not active and most of the I/O ports are configured in analog mode" (D64 p348). GPIOA_MODER resets to 0xABFF FFFF (D64 p355), so MODE5 and MODE7 are 11, analog mode. GPIOA_PUPDR resets to 0x6400 0000 (D64 p356); its only set bits are PUPD13/14/15 (the SWD pins), so PUPD5 and PUPD7 are 00, no pull-up or pull-down (D64 p357). D63 Table 12 has no note on PA5 or PA7 (D63 p53), and "unless otherwise specified by a note, all I/Os are set as floating inputs during and after reset" (D63 p50). So nothing in the MCU holds either line, and R38/R39 do not fight any internal state. The pins stay analog until firmware sets SPI1 alternate function. The AS5047P the AS5047P does not allow a floating digital input; for an unused SPI it asks for a pull-down on CLK and MOSI (D60 p4). Pull-down, not pull-up: the AS5047P uses SPI mode 1, CPOL=0, so CLK idles low (D60 p11), and the display samples SDA on the rising SCL edge (D67 p11), so a low idle clock suits it too. - 32-bit TIM2 counts the spindle. TIM4 counts the table, TIM3 counts the UI encoder, and TIM1 drives the bridge.
- Index handling (#45): capture plus ISR, no hardware index reset. The G4 encoder index reset (IE bit in TIMx_ECR) only works with the index on tim_etr_in (D64 p1340). SPINDLE_I (PB10, TIM2_CH3) and HEIGHT_I (PB9, TIM4_CH4) are capture channels, not ETR. TIM2_ETR is only on PA0, PA5 and PA15, TIM4_ETR only on PA8 and PB3 (D63 p61–62), and all are in use. Firmware sets CH3 (TIM2) and CH4 (TIM4) to input capture on the index edge. The capture latches the counter into CCRx in hardware (D64 p1314), so the index position has no interrupt-latency error; the ISR only reads CCRx and stores it as the reference, or subtracts it from the count. At 20k rpm the spindle index is ~333 Hz. Not chosen: moving SPINDLE_A to PA0 and SPINDLE_I to PA15 (TIM2_ETR), because VBUS_SENSE would then need another ADC pin, for no gain over the captured value.
- TIM4 input filter (#62): set IC1F, IC2F and IC4F (HEIGHT_A, HEIGHT_B, HEIGHT_I) to 1100, fSAMPLING = fDTS/16 with N = 8 (D64 p1378), and CKD = 00, so tDTS = t_tim_ker_ck (D64 p1366). TIM4 is on APB1; with the APB1 prescaler at 1 the timer clock equals PCLK1 (D64 p281), 170 MHz here. A level change then counts only after 8 equal samples at 10.6 MHz, 0.75 µs, so shorter glitches are dropped. Fastest real edges: the AS5311 incremental output runs up to 650 mm/s (D61 p2, p25) at 1.95 µm per count (D61 p9), so 333 k counts/s, 3.0 µs from an A edge to the next B edge. 0.75 µs is a quarter of that. The largest setting, 1111 (fDTS/32, N = 8), gives 1.5 µs, half of 3.0 µs; 1100 keeps more margin in case the AS5311 does not space its edges evenly (D61 gives no minimum edge spacing). The index pulse is 1 LSB wide (D61 p12, Fig. 4), so at least 3.0 µs too. A and B get the same delay, so their order is kept; 0.75 µs is small next to the 384 µs incremental propagation delay (D61 p9). If the timer clock changes, recompute: filter time = 16 × 8 / f_tim. The 650 mm/s figure is the sensor limit; the real table moves much slower.
- TIM4 / SSI cross-check (#62): on every SSI read, not only at bring-up, compare the TIM4 count with the absolute position. The SSI gives 12 bits per 2 mm pole pair and A/B give 10 bits per pole pair (D61 p9), so (TIM4 mod 1024) − (D11:D0 >> 2) must stay constant. Learn that offset at the first valid read after the outputs are unlocked (4.2); do not assume it is zero. Use only frames with OCF = 1, COF = 0 and even parity; skip frames with LIN = 1, as D11:D0 may then be wrong (D61 p14–15). Compare only when the table is still (TIM4 unchanged since the last read), because the incremental output lags by up to 384 µs and the absolute output by up to 96 µs (D61 p9). Allow ±3 counts: 2 LSB incremental hysteresis plus ±0.97 LSB DNL (D61 p9). Beyond that, wrap the difference to ±512 counts, correct TIM4 by it, count the event and show a warning. The SSI repeats every 2 mm, so an error of 1 mm (512 counts) or more cannot be corrected this way; it needs a re-zero.
- Using PC13–PC15 only as inputs and CSn-style slow outputs is deliberate. Check their drive limits in D63 before driving any load from them.

### 4.4 DRV8874 interface

- PMODE: tie high for PWM mode (IN1/IN2). PMODE is latched when nSLEEP goes high (D56 p10).
- nSLEEP, EN/IN1 and PH/IN2 have internal pull-downs (D56 p3).
- nFAULT is open drain, 5 mA max; pull up to 3.3 V (D56 p3, p4).
- VREF range is 0–3.6 V (D56 p4). I_TRIP = V_VREF / (R_IPROPI × A_IPROPI) (D56 p13). A_IPROPI = 450 µA/A typ, error ±5.5 % at 2–4 A (D56 p6 table). TI's worked examples use 455 (p13, p21).
- R_IPROPI = 1.33 kΩ 1 %, made as R5 1 kΩ + R25 330 Ω in series (C11702 + C25104, both Basic 1 %; no Basic 1.33 kΩ exists). Full-scale DAC (3.3 V) gives 5.5 A typ, or 5.85 A at −5.5 % gain (A_ERR is only specified for 2–4 A, D56 p6, so the −5.5 % is extrapolated). That is too close to the 6 A OCP minimum, so firmware caps DRV_VREF well below full scale (next item, #58). At 5.2 A stall, IPROPI ≈ 3.1 V, inside the ADC range.
- DRV_VREF cap (issue #58). The ITRIP comparator ignores IPROPI for t_BLK 1.1 µs after an input edge and then needs t_DEG 0.6 µs (D56 p6, p13), so the peak can overshoot ITRIP by up to about V_L / L × 1.7 µs, where V_L = V_VM − E − I·R is the voltage across the winding inductance (general knowledge). Past I_OCP (6 A min, 10 A typ) for t_OCP 3 µs, U5 shuts the outputs off, pulls nFAULT low (TIM1_BKIN stop) and retries after t_RETRY 2 ms (D56 p6, Table 6 p13). L is not measured yet (#16), so the cap is an interim value:
  - Cap: V_DRV_VREF ≤ 2.69 V, which is ITRIP = 2.69 V / (1.33 kΩ × 450 µA/A) = 4.5 A typ (4.45 A with TI's 455 µA/A, D56 p13). Tolerance: A_ERR ±5.5 % (D56 p6; extrapolated above 4 A), R_IPROPI ±1 %, measured VDDA ±0.7 % (4.7) give about 4.2–4.85 A. Worst-case margin to the 6 A OCP minimum is about 1.15 A.
  - That margin covers the overshoot for any L ≥ 37 µH even with V_L at the full 25 V bus (25 V × 1.7 µs / 1.15 A). With E ≥ 0 and R ≥ 4.66 Ω (section 2, cold), V_L at the trip point is at most 25 V − 4.2 A × 4.66 Ω ≈ 5.4 V, so L ≥ 8 µH is enough (general knowledge; R is a rough multimeter value).
  - Cost: stall current is limited to about 4.5 A instead of the 5.2 A cold stall, so about 13 % less stall torque until the cap is raised.
  - **Firmware rule (#58): DRV_VREF never exceeds 2.69 V. The cap is in volts and is converted to a DAC code with the measured VDDA (code = V × 4096 / VDDA, section 4.7), so it is about code 3490 at 3.159 V VDDA, not a fixed code. Every current limit (bit preset, I²t fold-back, section 5) is clamped to it.**
  - Bring-up: with the motor locked in the press, set the cap and scope TP13 (IPROPI) and DRV_IN2 (the PWM input, section 4.6) at 24 V. Read the peak current (V_IPROPI / (1.33 kΩ × 450 µA/A)) and the overshoot ΔI above ITRIP. Also do this from standstill and at the first on-pulses after t_OFF. Then set the final cap so that cap_typ × 1.08 + ΔI + 0.3 A ≤ 6 A (1.08 is the high tolerance above; 0.3 A is a margin chosen here, not from a datasheet), and never above 3.3 V on DRV_VREF. With ΔI = 0.3 A that allows about 5.0 A typ. Update this item with the measured ΔI and L (#16).
- DAC and ADC full scale follow +3V3 (VDDA); firmware scales DRV_VREF and IPROPI by the measured VDDA (section 4.7, #66).
- At reset the DAC is off and R4 (100 k) holds VREF at 0 V. The bridge then cannot drive current until firmware sets the limit, so it fails safe.
- IMODE = GND through R2 0 Ω (C17168, Basic): fixed off-time chopping, automatic retry after overcurrent, and nFAULT reports overcurrent only (D56 Table 6, p13). Fixed off-time supports 100 % duty (D56 p14). Not 20 kΩ (cycle-by-cycle): there nFAULT also goes low on every chop (D56 p14), so TIM1_BKIN would stop all TIM1 outputs, LCD_BL included, at each current limit, and 100 % duty is not possible (issue #30). R2 stays as a 0 Ω part so 20 kΩ or 62 kΩ can be fitted later.
- C14 (10 nF on IPROPI) is DNP. D56 p13 suggests it only if transients trigger the chopping early.
- IPROPI clamp (issue #33): D9 RB751V-40 (C7502691, Preferred, SOD-323), anode on IPROPI, cathode on +3V3. PA1 is TT_a (D63 p52), max 4.0 V (D63 p69). Without the clamp, IPROPI goes over 4 V above about 6.7 A, and OCP trips at 6 A min / 10 A typ only after t_OCP 3 µs (D56 p6), on every retry. IPROPI itself is rated to 5.75 V (D56 p4). IPROPI is a current output, so the diode alone limits the voltage. There is no series resistor, so PA1 keeps full resolution and the same source impedance. Vf ≤ 0.37 V at 1 mA, about 0.4 V at 10 mA (D85 p2), so PA1 stays at or below about 3.7 V. In normal running VREF comes from the DAC, so VREF ≤ +3V3, IPROPI ≤ +3V3, and the diode is off. Leakage is < 1 nA at 25 °C and about 10 nA at 100 °C (D85 p2), ≤ 13 µV across 1.33 kΩ. Capacitance is about 15 pF at 0 V (D85 p2), which adds about 20 ns. Layout: place D9 next to PA1.
- IPROPI sampling (issues #49, #57). IPROPI only mirrors current that flows drain to source in a low-side FET. In coast the current freewheels source to drain and IPROPI reads 0 (D56 p12). With drive/brake PWM (section 4.6) the OUT2 low-side FET carries the winding current drain to source both in forward drive and in brake (D56 p10, Fig. 10, paths 1 and 2), so it stays on and IPROPI follows the motor current through the whole period. D56 says the mirror senses in both drive and brake low-side slow decay (p12), and that t_DELAY then has no impact on IPROPI (p13). So there is no minimum duty for a valid sample. t_DELAY = 1.6 µs typ (D56 p6, Fig. 1 p7; no max given) applies only when the bridge leaves coast or sleep. PA1 (ADC12_IN2) is a fast channel; with R_IPROPI 1.33 kΩ, 12 bits needs 24.5 cycles = 408 ns at 60 MHz (R_AIN ≤ 1.5 kΩ, D63 p121). Trigger latency is ≤ 2.5 ADC clocks (D63 p119), negligible.
  - **Firmware rule: trigger the IPROPI conversion from TIM1_CH4 (free; tim1_cc4 is ADC1/2 injected trigger JEXTSEL = 1, D64 p381), synchronised to the DRV_IN2 PWM. Sample at the centre of the on-time (DRV_IN2 low) or of the off-time; in continuous conduction both equal the average current (general knowledge). After the bridge leaves coast or sleep, ignore samples for t_DELAY plus margin.** If the current reaches 0 inside the period (light load, section 4.6), the sample is no longer the average; use the model current (from V_BUS, duty, Kₑ·ω̂, R, L) there.
  - Bring-up: scope DRV_IN2 and TP13 (IPROPI). Check that IPROPI stays continuous across the drive/brake edges, and measure the real t_DELAY after coast.
  - If C14 (10 nF) is ever fitted, IPROPI gets τ = 1.33 kΩ × 10 nF ≈ 13 µs (general knowledge), about half a 40 kHz period. The ADC then reads a filtered value, not the instant current, and ITRIP reacts later; recheck this note.
- nSLEEP has an internal 100 kΩ pull-down (D56 p5), so it gets no external pull-down.
- Hardware stop (issue #11), on the MCU sheet: Q4 AO3400A (C20917, Basic) pulls DRV_nSLEEP to GND when ENABLE_IN is high (SW2 at stop), whatever the MCU does. Gate on ENABLE_IN, drain on DRV_nSLEEP, source on GND. PB2 drives DRV_nSLEEP through R28 1 kΩ 0402 (C11702, Basic), so PB2 high against Q4 on draws 3.3 mA instead of a short. Numbers: Q4 V_GS(th) ≤ 1.45 V and R_DS(on) ≤ 48 mΩ at V_GS 2.5 V (D82 p2), so 3.3 V on the gate turns it fully on; nSLEEP is then ≈ 0 V, under V_IL max 0.8 V (D56 p5). With Q4 off and PB2 high, nSLEEP sits at 3.3 V minus at most 75 µA × 1 kΩ = 75 mV, over V_IH min 1.5 V (D56 p5). The driver enters sleep within t_SLEEP 1 ms (D56 p5), and the motor coasts. R22/C31 (10 k, 100 nF) slow the gate by about 1 ms on the way to stop. Firmware still reads ENABLE_IN on PC14 and must also drop PB2.
- VM ripple (issue #34): C11, C42 and C43 are 10 µF 50 V X5R 1206 (C13585, Basic, netdaq part) on +24V at U5 VM, next to C10 100 nF. D56 p10 asks for 0.1 µF low-ESR ceramic plus "bulk capacitance, VM-rated", and p31 asks for low-ESR X5R/X7R placed close to VM. With drive/brake PWM (section 4.6, #57) the supply current is I during the on-time and 0 during the off-time, so the input ripple is about I·√(D(1−D)), 2.5 A rms at 5 A and 50 % duty (general knowledge). Drive/coast would roughly double it, since the winding current flows back into VM during the off-time (#57). C21 (Roqang RVT1H101M0607, 100 µF 50 V, 6.3 × 7.7 mm) is rated 114 or 130 mA rms at 120 Hz, 105 °C (the 50 V 100 µF 6.3 × 7.7 row has both, D86 p2), times 1.40 at ≥ 10 kHz (D86 p1), so about 0.16–0.18 A. That is far under the stall ripple. Not verified: that a stall is short, or that C21 survives a short overload (D86 says nothing about overload or how fast it heats). D86 gives no ESR at 20–40 kHz, so how the ripple splits between the ceramics and C21 is not known: the ceramics may take most of it, or C21 may run hot. Once #16 gives the running current, and at bring-up during a stall, measure C21's ripple current or temperature rise. Replace C21 with a higher-ripple part if it is over. Layout: C11, C42, C43 next to U5 pin 11 (VM) with a short loop to pin 9 (PGND).
- Continuous current / thermal limit for U5 (issue #40). ITRIP (VREF) only caps the peak; current chopping can hold a stall at ITRIP for as long as firmware lets it, so the heat limit is a firmware job. Numbers:
  - Losses: P = I² × (R_DS(on)_HS + R_DS(on)_LS) + P_SW + P_VM (D56 p22 Eq. 11–19). R_DS(on) is 120 mΩ max per FET at 25 °C (D56 p6) and about 1.5× that at 150 °C (Fig. 6/7, D56 p8), so the hot worst case is 0.36 Ω for the path. P_SW = 0.5 × 24 V × I × (150 + 150 ns) × f_PWM = 0.072 W/A at 20 kHz, double at 40 kHz (t_RISE/t_FALL D56 p6). P_VM ≈ 0.1 W (D56 p22 Eq. 10).
  - Target: T_J ≤ 150 °C, 10 °C under the 160 °C min thermal shutdown (D56 p6). Assumed T_A 40 °C inside the case (not measured).
  - R_θJA depends on the board, not the 36 °C/W in the thermal table (D56 p5, p22). TI's simulation, 1 oz copper (D56 p24 Fig. 21 and 23): 2-layer 63 °C/W at 4 cm² top copper, 44 °C/W at 16 cm²; 4-layer 31.5 °C/W at 4 cm², 30.4 °C/W at 16 cm².
  - Continuous limit at 20 kHz: 2-layer about 2.0 A (4 cm²) to 2.5 A (16 cm²); 4-layer about 3.0 A. The stackup is not decided: the PCB file is 2-layer now, and the choice waits for the running current from #16. If the drilling current is above the 2-layer limit, go 4-layer; if it is above about 3 A, see the DRV8873 fallback in the motor driver row of the section 4 table.
  - TI's model has thermal vias on a 1.2 mm grid under the whole pad (D56 p22–23); our footprint has 4 (#15). So the real limit is probably lower than these numbers (not quantified). Bring-up: run a known current for a few minutes and measure the U5 case temperature, then correct R_θJA.
  - Stall: 5.2 A gives about 10 W. Z_θJA reaches about 11 °C/W at about 0.3 s on every stackup (D56 p25 Fig. 25), so from 40 °C the junction reaches 150 °C in about 0.3 s. Thermal shutdown (D56 p17) is the backup, not the plan.
  - **Firmware rule: U5 I²t limit.** Estimate U5 power from the IPROPI current with the formula above, and T_J with a thermal model fitted to Z_θJA (D56 p25). Fold the current back to the continuous limit for the chosen stackup before T_J reaches 150 °C. A locked rotor (current at ITRIP, speed near 0) must cut the drive within 0.2 s. This is separate from the motor I²t in section 5; the lower of the two wins.

### 4.5 USB power path (issue #1)

- USB powers +3V3 through U8 so the MCU can be flashed and logged without the 24 V PSU.
- D8 sits in front of U8 so +3V3 cannot back-drive the host through U8 when only the PSU is on. U8 and the TPS5430 both only source current; whichever output is higher carries the load.
- PSU and USB both on (issue #56): U8 is 3.234–3.366 V (3.3 V ±2 %, D69 p1). The buck setpoint was lowered to 3.159 V nominal, 3.057–3.261 V worst case (see the Power row in section 3), so on most boards U8 is higher and carries the whole +3V3 load whenever USB is plugged in. The two ranges still overlap by 27 mV (3.234–3.261 V); on a board in that corner the buck carries part or all of the load, which only takes load off U8. With R10 10 k and two Basic/Preferred 1 % parts at the bottom, the only choice that keeps the buck fully under 3.234 V is 7.5 k ∥ 47 k (3.009–3.209 V), which leaves only 9 mV above the 3.0 V sensor minimum, so the overlap is accepted instead. U8 is 200 mA, 250 mW in SOT-23, and lists only a current limiter as protection, no thermal shutdown (D69 p1, p3), so the USB limits below must apply in this case too, not only when the PSU is off.
- USB only: +3V3 also back-feeds the TPS5430 output, and (general knowledge, not in D59) through the high-side FET body diode into +24V, which then sits near 2.7 V. The loads there are small (VBUS_SENSE divider, sleeping DRV8874, TPS5430 below UVLO). Accepted as is; no part was added to reg_3v3 for this.
- VBUS_SENSE is then about 2.7 V × 10/130 ≈ 0.2 V, so firmware can tell "no PSU".
- U8 limits: I_OUT ≥ 200 mA, dropout 250 mV typ / 680 mV max at 100 mA (D69 p5), 250 mW in SOT-23 (D69 p3), V_IN max 6.0 V (D69 p4).
- USB-only load budget: MCU 24.5 mA typ at 170 MHz, peripherals off (D63 p81), AS5047P 15 mA max (D60 p6), AS5311 21 mA max (D61 p7), backlight up to 61 mA (D67 p5, see 4.2), display logic not specified (D67 p6). About 150 mA worst case → ~0.24 W in U8 at 5 V in, right at the 250 mW limit.
- **Firmware rule (USB limits): whenever U8 may carry the load, cap the backlight and keep BUZZER (PB15) low (buzzer muted).** That is: when VBUS_SENSE shows no PSU, and also when the USB device is enumerated by a host (USB bus reset seen, device configured), even with the PSU on (#56). USB suspend counts as enumerated, because VBUS_USB stays on. Lift the limits only when the PSU is on and the DPPU retry (rule below) finds no host. With the PSU and USB both on, U8 is normally the higher regulator and carries the +3V3 load (see above). Without the backlight the load is about 90 mA, ~0.15 W in U8.
- Known gap (#56): a USB charger with no host gives VBUS_USB but no enumeration. VBUS_USB is not sensed (#44), so with the PSU on the firmware cannot see this case: the limits are off, and U8 can carry the whole load (~150 mA + ~63 mA buzzer), over its 200 mA (D69 p1). Accepted for a bench tool; do not leave the board on a USB charger while running from the PSU.
- Buzzer detail (issue #39): BZ1 is rated max 95 mA at 2700 Hz, 50 % duty, 5 V0-p (D75 p2). At 3.3 V that scales to roughly 63 mA (general knowledge, linear estimate). On top of the 150 mA budget that is over U8's 200 mA. The motor cannot run on USB only, so the buzzer has nothing to signal there. With the PSU on and a USB host attached (#56) the buzzer is muted too, so alerts must also show on the display. Not chosen: a series resistor (would also make it quieter on PSU power) and local bulk at BZ1 (+3V3 already has C17/C35/C36, 141 µF nominal).
- VBUS_USB is not sensed by the MCU (issue #44). No pin is free; PB1 (MOT_V2) was kept for back-EMF. On USB-only power a host is present by definition. With the PSU on, firmware cannot tell whether a host is there, so the internal D+ pull-up (DPPU, software controlled, D64 p2039) may feed an unpowered host, about 2 mA (general knowledge: ~1.5 kΩ pull-up into the host's 15 kΩ pull-down). USB 2.0 asks a self-powered device not to do this (general knowledge; the USB 2.0 spec is not in the RAG). Accepted, with the rule below.
- **Firmware rule: with the PSU on, set DPPU, and clear it again if no USB bus reset or SOF arrives within about 1 s. Retry every few seconds.** Back-drive into a powered-off host is then limited to short bursts. Not chosen: dropping MOT_V2 and putting a VBUS_USB divider on PB1.
- Shell pads of the EasyEDA C42400252 footprint were renumbered to pad 1 (SHELL) so all four connect to GND.

### 4.6 Braking energy (issue #3)

Decision: handle it in firmware. No brake resistor, no extra bulk capacitance.

- The PSU cannot sink current, so any energy the motor returns raises +24V. The bus must stay under the 40 V abs max of the DRV8874 (D56 p4) and the TPS5430 (D59 p4).
- Brake (IN1 = IN2 = 1) turns on both low-side FETs (D56 p11, Table 4). The motor is shorted through them, and the energy is burned in the winding and the FETs. Nothing flows to +24V.
- Current chopping also uses this low-side brake state (D56 p13), so it does not pump the bus either.
- Coast (IN1 = IN2 = 0) is Hi-Z (D56 p11). The back-EMF alone stays below the bus voltage, but the winding current does not stop when the bridge goes Hi-Z: the inductance keeps it flowing through the body diodes, the low side of one leg and the high side of the other, into VM (D56 p10, Fig. 10, path 3), until it has decayed (issue #57). One coast at a stop returns only the energy in the inductance, ½·L·I² (general knowledge). As the PWM off-state, coast would send the full winding current into +24V on every off-time: the bus current would swing between +I and −I, and IPROPI would read 0 during the off-time (D56 p12). So coast is used only to stop, never as the PWM off-state.
- Brake current is at most back-EMF / R ≈ 24 V / 4.66 Ω ≈ 5.2 A at full speed, the same as stall, and below the 6 A peak rating (D56 p4).
- The bus is pumped only when the bridge is driven with a duty below the back-EMF ratio while the low side recirculates (drive/brake PWM at low duty). Then the motor current reverses and flows into +24V on each on-time (general knowledge).
- **Firmware rule (#57): use drive/brake PWM. Hold DRV_IN1 high and PWM DRV_IN2: IN2 low is forward drive (OUT1 → OUT2), IN2 high is brake, low-side slow decay (D56 p11, Table 4). The off-time current then recirculates through the two low-side FETs (D56 p10, Fig. 10, path 2), and nothing flows to +24V. Keep the duty at or above Kₑ·ω̂ / V_BUS (V_BUS from VBUS_SENSE, scaled with the measured VDDA, section 4.7), so the average motor current never goes negative. Never command negative motor current. Slow down only with coast or full brake.** The current loop in section 5 clamps its reference at ≥ 0, and its back-EMF feedforward gives the duty floor.
- With drive/brake PWM the supply current is the winding current during the on-time and 0 during the off-time, so it never goes negative while the winding current stays positive. At light load the current ripple can be more than twice the average current; then the current reverses near the end of each off-time, and a little of it returns to +24V at the start of the next on-time. The average bus current still stays ≥ 0, so the bus is not pumped (general knowledge). Bring-up: at the lowest running load, scope TP13 (IPROPI) and DRV_IN2 and check whether the current reaches 0 inside the period. IPROPI cannot show the sign: a reversed current reads 0 or flows in the other low side (D56 p12, Eq. 1).
- **Firmware rule: if VBUS_SENSE reads above 30 V (2.31 V at the ADC), set the bridge to coast and log a fault.** The 2.31 V is in volts, converted with the measured VDDA, not a fixed ADC code (section 4.7, #66).
- Cost: no controlled regenerative deceleration. Stops are either coast (slow) or full brake (fast). That is enough for bit changes (section 6, Active brake).

### 4.7 ADC and DAC reference (issue #66)

Decision: no hardware change. Firmware measures VDDA with VREFINT and scales every ADC reading and DAC output by it.

- U2 VREF+ (pin 20) and VDDA (pin 21) are tied to +3V3. VREF+ is the reference for both the ADCs and the DACs, and VREF+ = VDDA is allowed (D63 p22). The DAC output is VREF+ × DOR / 4096 (D64 p726). The internal VREFBUF (2.048 / 2.5 / 2.9 V, D63 p22) cannot be used, because it drives the VREF+ pin, which is tied to VDDA.
- So the ADC and DAC full scale is whatever +3V3 is: 3.057–3.261 V from the buck, 3.234–3.366 V from U8 when USB is on (section 4.5, #56). It can change at run time, when USB is plugged in or out. Against an assumed 3.300 V that is −7.4 % to +2.0 %.
- What a fixed 3.300 V would do (VBUS_SENSE divider ratio 13, section 4.6): the 30 V overvoltage trip would land anywhere from 27.8 V (VDDA 3.057 V) to 30.6 V (VDDA 3.366 V). IPROPI current, the U5 and motor I²t limits (4.4, section 5) and DRV_VREF / ITRIP (4.4, #58) shift by the same −7.4 % to +2.0 %.
- DAC at full scale with VDDA 3.366 V: ITRIP = 3.366 V / (1.33 kΩ × 450 µA/A) = 5.62 A typ, or 5.95 A at −5.5 % gain (formula and A_IPROPI from 4.4, D56 p6, p13). That is under the 6 A peak rating by about 50 mA only, which is why firmware caps DRV_VREF at 2.69 V (4.4, #58).
- VREFINT is on ADC1_IN18 (D63 p30, D64 p674). Its factory value VREFINT_CAL is a 16-bit word at 0x1FFF 75AA–0x1FFF 75AB, taken at 30 °C ± 5 °C with VDDA = VREF+ = 3.0 V ± 10 mV (D63 p30). Then VDDA = 3.0 V × VREFINT_CAL / VREFINT_DATA (D64 p674). VREFINT is 1.182 / 1.212 / 1.232 V min / typ / max; its spread over temperature is ≤ 7.5 mV (about 0.6 %), and long-term drift ≤ 1000 ppm (D63 p74). So the measured VDDA is good to about ±0.7 %, plus the ADC's own error (not counted here).
- VREFINT needs ≥ 4 µs sampling time (D63 p74): at 60 MHz ADC clock that is 240 cycles, so use 247.5. The VREFEN bit in ADCx_CCR must be set to convert it (D64 p674). The buffer needs up to 12 µs to start after the ADC is enabled (D63 p74).
- **Firmware rule (VDDA scaling, #66): at start-up, before the first DAC write that enables the bridge and before any limit is checked, convert VREFINT several times and compute VDDA = 3.0 V × VREFINT_CAL / VREFINT_DATA. Repeat it at least every 10 ms in the ADC1 regular sequence (next to VBUS_SENSE; the injected IPROPI conversion keeps its timing), and low-pass the result. Convert every ADC reading to volts as code × VDDA / 4096 (VBUS_SENSE, IPROPI, MOT_V1/2), and compute every DAC code from the wanted voltage as V × 4096 / VDDA (DRV_VREF, and any internal DAC used as a COMP1 threshold). Never use a fixed 3.3 V. If VDDA is outside 3.0–3.4 V, coast the bridge and log a fault.** The 10 ms rate and the 3.0–3.4 V window are not from a datasheet: 10 ms is fast next to a USB plug event, and the window is the regulator ranges above with some margin.
- Bring-up: log VDDA on PSU only and on PSU plus USB, and compare with TP10 (+3V3) on a meter.

## 5. Control architecture

1. **Current loop** (PWM rate, 20–40 kHz): PI with back-EMF feedforward (Kₑ·ω̂). This loop sets torque.
2. **Observer** (about 10 kHz): disturbance observer that fuses the ABI speed with current and voltage, and outputs ω̂ and the load-torque estimate T̂_load.
3. **Load feedforward**: T̂_load / Kₜ added to the current reference. This is what reacts fastest when the bit bites.
4. **Speed loop** (1–2 kHz): PI that trims the remaining error and generates the setpoint ramp.
5. **Limits**: current/torque limit per bit preset, plus two thermal estimates (I²t): one for the motor winding and one for U5 DRV8874 (section 4.4, issue #40). The lower limit wins.

Parameter ID at startup: lock rotor briefly to get R and L, spin up to get Kₑ, coast down to get J and friction. Track R with temperature in operation.

## 7. Firmware structure

- `hal/`: PWM, ADC, encoder timer, SPI1 (AS5047P and ST7789 display, shared bus), height sensor
- `control/`: current PI, observer, speed PI, limits, parameter ID
- `process/`: chip-load, torque watchdog, contact/breakthrough, presets, counters
- `ui/`: display screens, encoder menu, alarms
- `log/`: UART/USB streaming of ω, i, T̂, height for tuning

Run control in ISRs triggered by the PWM timer; run UI and process logic in the main loop or a low-priority task.

### 7.1 UCPD dead-battery pull-downs (issue #19)

- After reset the STM32G431 can put Rd = 5.1 kΩ to GND on PB6 and PB4. A high level on PA9 turns it on for PB6, and a high level on PA10 turns it on for PB4 (D63 p60). The feature is on after reset; `PWR_CR3.UCPD1_DBDIS` (bit 14) = 1 turns it off, and RM0440 recommends turning it off in all cases (D64 p255).
- On this board PA9 = LCD_BL PWM, PA10 = DRV_IN2 PWM, PB6 = HEIGHT_A, PB4 = ENC_A. Without the fix, each high PWM period puts 5.1 kΩ on HEIGHT_A and ENC_A. ENC_A (10 k pull-up R18) would then read ≈ 3.3 V × 5.1 / 15.1 ≈ 1.1 V.
- **Firmware rule: set `PWR_CR3.UCPD1_DBDIS = 1` first thing at startup, before PA9 or PA10 is set up as an output or AF.** UCPD1 is not used, so nothing needs to be set up before it.
- Before firmware runs, PA9 and PA10 are in analog mode (GPIOA_MODER reset value 0xABFF FFFF, D64 p355), and each net is held low: LCD_BL by R17 100 kΩ to GND, DRV_IN2 by the DRV8874 internal 100 kΩ pull-down on PH/IN2 (D56 p3, p18 fig. 16). So the pull-downs stay off during reset and boot. No hardware change.
- Not checked: the ST system bootloader (BOOT0 high, or empty flash) may use PA9/PA10 as USART1 (general knowledge, not in D63/D64 as searched). That only matters while the bootloader runs; the motor driver stays asleep then, because nSLEEP is pulled low.

## 8. Phases

1. **Characterise the motor**: measure R, L, Kₑ, J and the no-load current on the bench, and log current while drilling real holes.
2. **Sensor bring-up**: mount the magnet, put an AS5047P on a breakout, and check speed ripple at 20k rpm (magnet centring).
3. **Board rev A**: MCU, driver, current sense, AS5047P connector, TFT display, encoder, debug and logging.
4. **Control loops**: current loop, then speed PI, then observer and feedforward. Tune with logged load-step tests.
5. **Height sensor**: fit it, calibrate it, add the depth readout.
6. **Process features**: chip-load and torque watchdog first, then auto-zero, breakthrough and presets.
7. **Refinement**: thermal tracking, wear log, optional haptic knob.

## 9. Risks

- Magnet runout or imbalance shows up as once-per-revolution speed ripple (333 Hz at 20k rpm). Needs a press-fit cap and filtering.
- Brush and commutator noise on the current signal. Filter it and sample synchronously with the PWM.
- Lever linkage is nonlinear with pin play, which is why the table is measured directly rather than at the lever.
- Printed head arm flexes under feed force. Small for micro bits, but the table sensor won't see it.
- Regenerative braking pumps the 24 V bus, because a barrel-jack PSU can't sink current. Handled by firmware rules in section 4.6.
- Hot-plugging the barrel jack causes inrush and a voltage spike. Decision (#37): no inrush limiter; the risk is accepted. Q1's body diode (drain on VIN_RAW, source on +24V) charges +24V at plug-in, so an RC on the Q1 gate cannot slow it, and real soft start would need a second FET or a hot-swap IC. +24V has only about 140 µF (C21 100 µF + C11, C15, C42, C43 4× 10 µF), which is a small charge at 24 V. The spike comes from cable inductance ringing with the low-ESR ceramics; C21's ESR damps it and D3 SMBJ24A clamps it to 38.9 V at 15.5 A (D66 p2), under the 40 V abs max of DRV8874 (D56 p4) and TPS5430 (D59 p4). The damping and the inrush peak are general knowledge, not measured. A series NTC was rejected: it drops power at the 5 A stall and does not limit on a quick re-plug while hot. Bring-up: scope +24V at TP9 while hot-plugging J1 with the bench PSU and its cable, and check that the peak stays under 40 V. If it does not, revisit this.
- The TPS5430 cannot hold its setpoint at light load above about 29–30 V on VIN (#51). Its minimum on-time can be as high as 200 ns, and Eq. 14 (D59 p17) gives V_OUTMIN = 0.12 × ((V_INMAX − I_OMIN × 0.110) + V_D) − I_OMIN × R_L − V_D. At I_OMIN ≈ 0 and V_D 0.5 V, the 3.159 V nominal setpoint (#56) needs V_IN ≤ 3.659 / 0.12 − 0.5 ≈ 30.0 V, and the lowest worst-case setpoint 3.057 V needs V_IN ≤ 29.1 V. D3 SMBJ24A conducts from 26.7–29.5 V (V_BR) and clamps at up to 38.9 V (D66 p2), so during a surge VIN can sit above 30 V, and above the 36 V recommended maximum (D59 p4), while staying under the 40 V absolute maximum (D59 p4). +3V3 may then rise for the length of the surge; how far depends on its length and the 141 µF output cap (not calculated). Accepted: with the PSU at ≤ 25 V (section 2) VIN only gets there during a transient, never in steady state.
- No input fuse between J1/J11 and +24V. Decision (#38): the PSU's own short-circuit protection is relied on. If D3, Q1 or U5 fails short, the PSU's limit current flows through J1 (5 A, D78 p1) and the board copper until the PSU shuts down. A fuse was rejected: it must carry the 5.2 A cold stall, so it would be about 10 A, and that does not blow at the 6–9 A a 150 W brick gives into a short (general knowledge), so it would not protect J1. JLC has no Basic or Preferred fuse (jlcsearch, 2026-11-22), so it would also add a feeder fee. A PTC was rejected: the only ≥ 5 A SMD part found (2920L500/30GR, C19078763) holds 5 A, below the stall, and is rated 30 V, below the 38.9 V TVS clamp. So the PSU must have short-circuit/overcurrent protection (hiccup or foldback), see the supply item in section 2. A lab supply must have its current limit set to 6 A or less.
- No ESD protection on the sensor cables J7 (table) and J6 (spindle). Decision (#43): not needed. Both cables are internal wiring inside the press; they are plugged once at assembly and only touched during service, so they are not user-facing ports like USB (which has D5/D6/D7). The MCU and sensor pins have their own on-chip ESD structures for handling (general knowledge, not checked against a datasheet). Rule: plug and unplug J7 and J6 with power off. A TVS array would add a part, likely an Extended feeder fee, and capacitance on CLK/DO. Revisit only if a sensor or MCU pin fails in use.
- Nothing is fitted across the motor connector J3 (MOTOR_A/MOTOR_B): no ESD part and no cap. Decision (#50). ESD: the motor lead is internal wiring, like J6/J7 (#43), and the bridge outputs swing to 24 V and take inductive kicks anyway. Cap: the DRV8874 typical application has nothing on OUT1/OUT2 (D56 p20). A cap at the bridge is charged hard on every PWM edge, about C·V²·f = 100 nF × 24² × 40 kHz ≈ 2.3 W of extra FET loss, and its charge spike lands in the IPROPI sample window (4.4) and adds to the OCP current (general knowledge, not from a datasheet). If commutator noise is a problem at bring-up (EMI, or noise on IPROPI or the sensors), solder a small cap on the motor terminals instead, where the cable inductance separates it from the bridge, and recheck the bridge temperature.
- AS5047P propagation delay at high speed. Use ABI with DAEC enabled.
